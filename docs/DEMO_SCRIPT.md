# Demo video script (target 2:45, hard limit 3:00)

Record the deployed site in a desktop browser with Screen Studio. Pre-load the page once so the sample photos are cached. Speak over the actions; the lines below are a guide, not a script to read word for word.

| Time | On screen | Say (roughly) |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | The start page | "Renovation ideas online ignore climate. A drought-tolerant yard makes sense in Irvine and none in Minneapolis. EcoReno edits a photo of a real house for the climate of any U.S. city, and shows the numbers behind every change." |
| 0:15–0:30 | Sample homes tab, click the Cape Cod | "Judges can pick a real house here. These are free-licensed photos, credited to the photographers. You can also search for one or upload your own." |
| 0:30–0:50 | City search: type "Portland" to show Oregon and Maine, then choose Irvine, CA. Scroll the climate panel | "Any U.S. city. This is ten years of daily weather for Irvine: it's semi-arid, plants need about 36 more inches of water a year than falls as rain, and eight months are dry." |
| 0:50–1:05 | Feature chips, then the recommendation cards; open one score breakdown | "The vision model found the lawn, the roof and the windows. Each suggestion is scored from the climate evidence and what's actually in the photo, and every card shows its numbers." |
| 1:05–1:40 | Click "Edit my photo". Drag the slider, hover a pin, open the change map, scroll the change log | "Same photo, edited: native planting where the lawn was, solar on the roof plane that faces the camera, awnings on the front windows. A second model checked the edit, and it flags what's only partly done instead of hiding it. The change map is a plain pixel comparison, no AI, showing exactly what changed." |
| 1:40–2:15 | Compare another city: Minneapolis. Show the comparison table and the three panels | "Same house, Minneapolis: 149 freezing nights and heavier storms, so it gets a windbreak, a rain garden and a rain barrel, and a reflective roof is blocked because it would raise winter heating. The data picks the design, not the city's name." |
| 2:15–2:35 | Open "How this image was made" | "The image model never sees a number or a city name, only concrete instructions plus what to keep, so it can't fall back on stereotypes. The pipeline is geocoding, ten years of climate data, scoring, an edit plan, FLUX Kontext for the edit, and Gemini for the review." |
| 2:35–2:50 | Back to the top, then the GitHub repo | "It's a concept tool, not a site survey or an appraisal. Code and data sources are on GitHub. Thanks for watching." |

If the Minneapolis result isn't pre-generated yet, generate it live before recording and keep the take, or cut that section to 15 seconds using the comparison table alone.
